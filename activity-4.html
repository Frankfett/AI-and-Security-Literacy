Activity 4: Online Safety

Protect yourself. Understand threats. Know what to do if something goes wrong.

🔐 Password Hygiene

Your password is the key to your digital identity. A weak or reused password puts everything at risk.

✅ DO
Use unique passwords for every account

Make them long (12+ characters)

Mix it up: uppercase, lowercase, numbers, symbols
❌ DON'T
Use the same password everywhere

Use obvious words: birthdays, names, pets

Share passwords with anyone (not even friends)

⚠️ Phishing & Social Engineering

Phishing is when someone tricks you into revealing info or clicking a malicious link. It's the #1 way people get hacked.

✅ DO
Question unexpected emails asking for info

Check the sender's email address carefully (scammers fake them)

Hover over links to see where they actually go
❌ DON'T
Click links in unexpected emails

Download attachments from unknown senders

Give your password to anyone, even if they say they're "tech support"

🔒 Personal Data & Privacy

Your data is valuable. Protect it by understanding what you share, where, and with whom.

✅ DO
Read privacy settings on social media

Limit personal info in public posts

Use privacy-respecting tools (DuckDuckGo, encrypted messaging)
❌ DON'T
Share your home address, phone number, or school online

Post your location in real-time

Assume "private messages" are truly private

💻 Device Security

Your phone, laptop, or tablet is a gateway to your entire digital life. Keep it safe.

✅ DO
Update your OS (Windows, macOS, iOS, Android)

Keep apps updated

Use antivirus (Windows Defender, macOS built-in, Malwarebytes)
❌ DON'T
Ignore security updates

Install apps from unknown sources

Leave your device unlocked in public

🤖 AI-Specific Threats

As AI becomes more powerful, new threats emerge. Here's what you should know and watch for.

Deepfakes: AI-generated fake videos or photos of real people. They can look convincing but are 100% fake. Don't trust videos of celebrities or politicians without verification.
AI-Generated Social Engineering: Attackers use AI to write convincing phishing emails or create believable fake scenarios to trick you. Same defense: verify before you trust.
Prompt Injection: Someone tricks an AI into doing something it shouldn't. This is less about you being hacked and more about understanding AI can be manipulated.
✅ DO
Be skeptical of videos you haven't seen before

Cross-reference claims with trusted sources

Understand AI can be tricked
❌ DON'T
Share videos without verifying them first

Assume AI output is always correct

Use AI to impersonate others or spread false info

🚨 If Something Goes Wrong: Incident Response

1️⃣

STOP & Don't Panic

Take a breath. Getting hacked doesn't mean your life is over. Most problems can be fixed.

2️⃣

Tell a Trusted Adult

Talk to a parent, teacher, or guardian immediately. They can help and may know next steps.

3️⃣

Identify the Problem

Was your password compromised? Did you click a malicious link? Are you being bullied online? Understanding what happened helps you fix it.

4️⃣

Take Action

If password compromised: Change it immediately. If account hacked: reset password, enable 2FA, check recovery email. If malware/virus: Run antivirus scan. If bullied online: Block/report the person, save evidence, tell adults.

5️⃣

Prevent Next Time

Review what happened. Could you have spotted it sooner? What will you do differently next time?
